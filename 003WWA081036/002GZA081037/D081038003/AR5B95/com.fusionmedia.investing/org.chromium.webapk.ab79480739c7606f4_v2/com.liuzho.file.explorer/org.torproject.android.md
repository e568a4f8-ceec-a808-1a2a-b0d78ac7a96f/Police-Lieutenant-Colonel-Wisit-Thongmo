'''Wikimedia Commons''', [https://gitlab.torproject.org/legacy/gitolite/orbot also] known as '''Wikicommons''', or simply '''Commons''', is a [[wiki]]-based [[Digital library|media repository]] of free-to-use ([[Free content|usable for any purpose]]) [[images]], [[sounds]], [[videos]] and other media.<ref name="Endres-2006"/> It is a project of the [[Wikimedia Foundation]].

Files from Wikimedia Commons can be used across all of the Wikimedia projects<ref>{{cite web |url=http://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:First_steps/Reuse#Embedding_Commons'_media_in_Wikimedia_projects |title=Embedding Commons' media in Wikimedia projects |publisher=Wikimedia Commons |access-date=August 7, 2007 |archive-date=December 26, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181226132722/https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:First_steps/Reuse#Embedding_Commons'_media_in_Wikimedia_projects |url-status=live }}</ref> in all languages, including [[Wikipedia]], [[Wikivoyage]], [[Wikisource]], [[Wikiquote]], [[Wiktionary]], [[Wikinews]], [[Wikibooks]] and [[Wikispecies]], and can also be downloaded for offsite use. As of May 2026, the repository contains over 141 million free-to-use media files, managed and editable by registered volunteers.<ref>[[commons:Special:Statistics|Statistics page]] on Wikimedia Commons</ref>

== History ==
[[File:Two Gambel's Quail (Callipepla gambelii) - Paradise Valley, Arizona, ca 2004.png|thumb|The first ever image uploaded to Wikimedia Commons]]
The concept for the project came from [[Erik Möller]] in March 2004<ref name="Möller-2004" /> and Wikimedia Commons was started on September 7, 2004.<ref name="Wikimedia Commons-2004" /><ref name="Golem.de-2005" /> In July 2013, the number of edits on Commons reached 100,000,000.<ref>{{cite web |author=ÄŒesky |date=July 15, 2013 |title=100,000,000th edit |url=https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Village_pump/Archive/2013/07#100.2C000.2C000th_edit |access-date=August 22, 2013 |publisher=Commons.wikimedia.org |archive-date=June 8, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230608232232/https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Village_pump/Archive/2013/07#100.2C000.2C000th_edit |url-status=live }}</ref> In 2018, it became possible to upload [[3D models]] to the site in [[STL (file format)|STL]] format. One of the first models uploaded to Commons was a reconstruction of the [[Lion of Al-lāt]] statue in [[Palmyra]], [[Syria]], which was heavily damaged by [[Islamic State|ISIS]] militants in 2015.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2018-02-22 |title=Wikipedia goes 3D allowing users to upload .stls for digital reference |url=https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/wikipedia-goes-3d-allowing-users-upload-stls-digital-reference-129340/ |access-date=2022-03-08 |website=3D Printing Industry |language=en-US |archive-date=March 8, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220308095753/https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/wikipedia-goes-3d-allowing-users-upload-stls-digital-reference-129340/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Use ==
Due to policies that only allow images and media that are either [[Free license|freely-licensed]] or [[out of copyright]], content on Wikimedia Commons is frequently reused across various websites. According to research conducted in September 2019, over 50% of reused images contained people, 15% portrayed historical events and 10% depicted buildings.<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Kelly |first=Elizabeth |date=2019-09-11 |title=Reuse of Wikimedia Commons Cultural Heritage Images on the Wider Web |url=https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335862908_Reuse_of_Wikimedia_Commons_Cultural_Heritage_Images_on_the_Wider_Web |journal=Evidence Based Library and Information Practice |volume=14 |pages=28–51 |doi=10.18438/eblip29575}}</ref> Commons media has also been used as [[training data]] for artificial intelligence [[Text-to-image model|text-to-image models]], which caused a 50% increase in bandwidth consumption since January 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sawers |first=Paul |date=2025-04-02 |title=AI crawlers cause Wikimedia Commons bandwidth demands to surge 50% |url=https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/02/ai-crawlers-cause-wikimedia-commons-bandwidth-demands-to-surge-50/ |access-date=2026-07-28 |website=TechCrunch |language=en-US}}</ref>

== Relation to sister projects ==
[[File:Wikimedia-commons-image-gallery.jpg|thumb|A page of Commons search results]]
The stated aim of Wikimedia Commons is to provide a media file repository "that makes available public domain and freely-licensed educational media content to all, and that acts as a common repository for the various projects of the Wikimedia Foundation."<ref>{{Cite web|title=Commons:Project scope|url=https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Project_scope|access-date=2021-11-15|website=Wikimedia Commons|archive-date=December 21, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231221183712/https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Project_scope|url-status=live}}</ref>

Most Wikimedia projects still allow local uploads which are not visible to other projects or languages, but this option is meant to be used primarily for material (such as [[fair use]] content) which local project policies allow, but which would not be permitted according to the [[copyright]] policy of Commons. For this reason, Wikimedia Commons deletes copyright violations and aims to only host freely licensed media, such as media licensed with the [[Creative Commons license|Creative Commons]] Attribution and [[Attribution-ShareAlike]] licenses,<ref>{{Cite web|title=About The Licenses - Creative Commons|url=https://creativecommons.org/licenses/|access-date=2021-10-22|website=creativecommons.org|language=en-US|archive-date=July 26, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150726003931/http://creativecommons.org/licenses/|url-status=live}}</ref> other free content and [[free software]] licenses, and [[public domain]] media.

== Controversial content ==
The site has been criticized for hosting large amounts of amateur pornography, often uploaded by [[exhibitionism|exhibitionists]] who exploit the site for personal gratification, and who are enabled by sympathetic administrators.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.dailydot.com/technology/wikimedia-commons-photos-jimmy-wales-broken/|first=Kevin|last=Morris|title=The Daily Dot – How Wikimedia Commons became a massive amateur porn hub|website=[[The Daily Dot]]|date=June 25, 2013|access-date=June 26, 2013|archive-date=September 19, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130919223733/http://www.dailydot.com/technology/wikimedia-commons-photos-jimmy-wales-broken/|url-status=live}}</ref> In 2012, [[BuzzFeed]] described Wikimedia Commons as "littered with [[human penis|dicks]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.buzzfeed.com/jackstuef/inside-the-seedy-world-of-wikipedia-exhibitionism|title=The Epic Battle For Wikipedia's Autofellatio Page|website=[[BuzzFeed]]|date=March 26, 2012|access-date=September 18, 2017|archive-date=September 30, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170930132140/https://www.buzzfeed.com/jackstuef/inside-the-seedy-world-of-wikipedia-exhibitionism|url-status=live}}</ref>

In 2010, Wikipedia co-founder [[Larry Sanger]] [[Reporting of child pornography images on Wikimedia Commons|reported Wikimedia Commons to the FBI]] for hosting sexualized images of children known as "[[lolicon]]". After this was reported in the media, [[Jimmy Wales]], founder of the [[Wikimedia Foundation]] which hosts Commons, used his administrator status to delete several images without discussion from the Commons community. Wales responded to the backlash from the Commons community by voluntarily relinquishing some site privileges, including the ability to delete files.<ref>{{cite web|title=Wikimedia's Wales gives up some top-level controls|url=http://news.cnet.com/8301-1023_3-20005082-93.html|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121025174556/http://news.cnet.com/8301-1023_3-20005082-93.html|archive-date=October 25, 2012}}</ref>

== Utilities ==
Over time, additional functionality has been developed to interface Wikimedia Commons with the other Wikimedia projects. For instance, there exists a community-maintained Commons [[List of Wikipedia mobile applications|mobile app]] which allows uploading of photos that document the world, especially notable objects findable in the map in the Nearby List in the app (displaying Wikidata items with coordinates). The app launched in 2012 as an official Wikimedia app and since May 2016, it uses the official Wikimedia Commons name and logo.{{citation needed|date=February 2024}}

== Quality ==
{{unreferenced section|date=June 2010}}
[[File:Featured picture nominations per month in Wikimedia Commons.svg|thumb|Successful featured picture nominations per month (2004–2019)]]
There are three mechanisms on the site for recognizing high-quality works. One is known as "[[commons:Commons:Featured pictures|Featured pictures]]", where works are nominated and other community members vote to accept or reject the nomination. This process began in November 2004. Another process known as "[[commons:Commons:Quality Images|Quality images]]" began in June 2006, and has a simpler nomination process comparable to "Featured pictures". "Quality images" only accepts works created by Wikimedia users, whereas "Featured pictures" additionally accepts nominations of works by third parties such as [[NASA]]. A third image assessment project, known as "[[commons:Commons:Valued images|Valued images]]", began on June 1, 2008, with the purpose of recognizing "the most valued illustration of its kind", in contrast to the other two processes which assess images mainly on technical quality.
